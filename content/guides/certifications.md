## Why add a certification?

An actuarial qualification is a deep credential on its own. A complementary certification can still strengthen your profile for a specific direction: investments and asset management, financial risk and ERM, or corporate finance and valuation. The three below are the ones most often paired with actuarial work.

Fees, exam windows, formats and eligibility rules for all three change regularly. The details below describe each programme's general structure. Check the official website for current costs, dates and requirements before you register.

## Chartered Financial Analyst (CFA)

**Offered by:** [CFA Institute](https://www.cfainstitute.org)

### Structure

- **Levels:** three (I, II and III), taken in sequence.
- **Format:** Level I is multiple choice. Level II uses item sets (vignette-based questions). Level III combines item sets with constructed-response (essay) questions.
- **Exam windows:** offered in several testing windows a year, with fewer windows for the higher levels.
- **Time commitment:** the longest of the three. Completing all levels typically takes several years.
- **Cost:** the most expensive of the three. You pay a one-off enrolment fee plus a registration fee for each level, and registering early is cheaper.

### Why it's valuable for actuaries

- It gives a deep grounding in investment analysis and portfolio management.
- It is highly recognised in asset management and investment banking.
- It complements actuarial work on insurer investment portfolios and pension funds.
- It brings global recognition and a large professional network.
- It places a strong emphasis on ethics and professional standards.

### Requirements for the charter

- Meet the entry criteria to register: broadly, a bachelor's degree, final-year enrolment, or an equivalent combination of work and education.
- Pass all three levels.
- Complete the required amount of qualifying work experience. Check CFA Institute for the current definition.
- Become a regular member of CFA Institute, which includes providing professional references.

## Financial Risk Manager (FRM)

**Offered by:** [Global Association of Risk Professionals (GARP)](https://www.garp.org)

### Structure

- **Parts:** two, Part I and Part II.
- **Format:** multiple choice in both parts.
- **Exam windows:** a small number of fixed windows each year. Historically these were May and November, so check GARP for the current schedule.
- **Time commitment:** shorter than the CFA. Many candidates complete it in around one to two years.
- **Cost:** mid-range. You pay a one-off enrolment fee plus a registration fee for each part, with early-registration discounts.

### Why it's valuable for actuaries

- It focuses specifically on risk management and quantitative analysis.
- It applies directly to actuarial risk-modelling work.
- It places a strong emphasis on market, credit and operational risk.
- It is particularly useful for actuaries in enterprise risk management (ERM).
- It needs less time than the CFA.

### Requirements for certification

- Pass both parts.
- Demonstrate two years of full-time professional experience in financial risk management or a related field, within GARP's time limits.

## Financial Modeling and Valuation Analyst (FMVA)

**Offered by:** [Corporate Finance Institute (CFI)](https://corporatefinanceinstitute.com)

### Structure

- **Format:** an online, self-paced programme of courses, each with its own assessment, plus a final exam.
- **Time commitment:** typically a few months, depending on your pace.
- **Access and cost:** by far the cheapest of the three. It is sold as a programme or a subscription, so check CFI's current plans and what level of course access each includes.

### Why it's valuable for actuaries

- It teaches practical Excel and financial-modelling skills.
- Its valuation techniques apply directly to actuarial and corporate finance work.
- Self-paced learning fits around a busy actuarial exam schedule.
- It gives hands-on experience with DCF models and M&A analysis.
- It is an affordable, flexible option.
- It is a good fit for actuaries moving into corporate finance roles.

### Requirements

- Complete all the required courses and their practical modelling assignments.
- Pass the course assessments and the final exam at CFI's required pass mark.

## Quick comparison

| Certification | Time commitment | Relative cost | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| CFA | Several years (three levels) | Highest | Investment analysis, asset management |
| FRM | Around one to two years (two parts) | Medium | Risk management, ERM roles |
| FMVA | A few months (self-paced) | Lowest | Financial modelling, valuation, corporate finance |

## Choosing well

- **Prioritise your actuarial exams.** A certification is a complement, not a substitute, and the study loads can clash.
- **Match it to your direction.** Choose the CFA for investments, the FRM for risk and ERM, and the FMVA for modelling and corporate finance skills you can apply quickly.
- **Ask about employer support.** Some employers fund certifications or give study leave for them, so ask before you pay.
