## Why keywords matter

Many employers run applications through an Applicant Tracking System (ATS) before a person reads them. The system parses your CV and compares it with the job description, so the right technical, actuarial and financial terms improve your chance of reaching a human. Keywords only help if the CV is also readable. Keep the formatting simple, as described in General CV Tips, and include terms naturally where they describe real work.

Use the lists below as a menu, not a checklist. Choose the terms that match both the job description and your genuine experience.

## Actuarial analyst

Use these for life, health, short-term (general) and long-term insurance, pensions, and consulting analyst roles.

- **Statistics and modelling:** probability, statistics, regression, time series, GLMs, predictive modelling
- **Multivariate methods:** variance, covariance, correlation, PCA
- **Machine learning:** machine learning, scikit-learn, TensorFlow, NumPy, pandas
- **Insurance:** claims, life insurance, long-term insurance, short-term insurance, pricing, valuation
- **Data:** data cleaning, outlier detection, financial analysis, financial reporting
- **Risk:** risk factors, sensitivity analysis, VaR, expected shortfall (ES), stress testing, scenario analysis
- **Tools:** SQL, Python, R, Excel, VBA, Prophet (actuarial projection software), actuarial and policy administration systems
- **Frameworks:** regulatory frameworks, IFRS (including IFRS 17), GAAP

## Quantitative modelling and research

Use these for quant research, quant developer and front-office modelling roles.

- **Stochastic calculus:** Itô's lemma, SDEs, Markov processes, martingales, conditional expectations
- **Models:** Heston, SABR, Bates, local volatility
- **Simulation:** Monte Carlo simulation, variance reduction
- **Learning and statistics:** statistical learning, feature engineering, hypothesis testing, cross-validation
- **Time series:** ARIMA, Kalman filter, state-space models
- **Strategy research:** factor models, alpha signals, backtesting, Sharpe ratio
- **Engineering:** Python, C++, optimisation, numerical methods

## Risk and XVA quant

Use these for counterparty credit risk, XVA, market risk and model validation roles.

- **Exposure:** counterparty risk, credit exposure, EE, EPE, PFE
- **Valuation adjustments:** CVA, DVA, FVA, MVA, KVA
- **Sensitivities and scenarios:** sensitivities, stress scenarios, risk-factor shocks
- **Rates:** interest rate curves, multi-curve framework
- **Simulation:** Monte Carlo, path simulation, forward simulation, scenario generators
- **Risk types:** liquidity risk, jump risk, wrong-way risk
- **Regulation:** FRTB, Basel III/IV
- **Validation:** model validation, benchmarking, independent review
- **Technology:** Python, SQL, C++, risk engines

## How to use these keywords

- **Be selective.** As a rule of thumb, aim for roughly 12 to 18 well-chosen keywords. Relevance beats quantity.
- **Place them in context.** Put them in your Skills section and in experience and project bullets, where they describe what you did, not in a hidden block or a list of buzzwords.
- **Mirror the job description.** If the advert says "IFRS 17" or "GLMs", use the same wording, as long as it is true of your experience.
- **Don't stuff.** Repeating terms unnaturally can count against you with both software and recruiters.
- **Mix categories.** Combine technical, actuarial and financial terms for a balanced profile.
- **Pair keywords with results.** "Built a GLM frequency model in Python that improved [metric] by [X]%" is far stronger than "GLM, Python".

## Final note

Relevant keywords can noticeably improve your chances of passing ATS screening, but they support your CV rather than replace its substance. Present your skills, experience and measurable achievements clearly, and weave in the most relevant terms naturally.
