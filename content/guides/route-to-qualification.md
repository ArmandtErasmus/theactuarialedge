## Two professional bodies, one shape of journey

Most actuarial students in South Africa qualify through the Actuarial Society of South Africa (ASSA). Students in the UK, and many internationally, qualify through the Institute and Faculty of Actuaries (IFoA). The two routes share the same shape:

1. Register as a student member.
2. Claim any exemptions your degree gives you.
3. Pass the core (foundation) exams.
4. Gain supervised work experience.
5. Specialise in a practice area.
6. Complete professionalism requirements.
7. Qualify, then keep up continuing professional development (CPD).

Exam names, codes and rules are revised from time to time. Treat the subject codes below as a guide, and confirm the current curriculum, exemption policy and experience requirements on the [ASSA](https://www.actuarialsociety.org.za) or [IFoA](https://actuaries.org.uk) website before you plan your exams.

## South Africa: the ASSA route

### 1. Choose your entry path

Most students enter through an accredited actuarial science degree, for example at UCT, UP, NWU, Wits or Stellenbosch. These degrees can earn exemptions from the A-series (associate-level) exams.

Students from non-traditional degrees such as mathematics, physics or statistics can also join. They may need strong marks and support from their university, so check ASSA's current student membership criteria.

### 2. Become a student member

Register with ASSA as a student member. If you studied an accredited degree, apply for the exemptions you are entitled to. This is also the time to map out your A-series exams and the normative skills pathway, which covers professionalism and communication training.

### 3. Exams and work-based learning

Work through the remaining A-series exams (the A100 and A200 subjects, for example A111 to A214) and A311, and complete the normative skills assignments.

At the same time, secure an actuarial trainee or analyst role and start Work-Based Learning (WBL). WBL is the mandatory practical-experience requirement, signed off by a supervisor, so it pays to begin it early.

### 4. Fellowship track

Pass the required Fellowship Principles subjects (the F100 series) and a Fellowship Applications subject (the F200 series). Check ASSA's rules for how many of each you need. These subjects define your practice area, for example:

- life insurance
- general (short-term) insurance
- retirement funds (pensions)
- health
- banking
- investments
- enterprise risk management

### 5. Qualify and keep developing

Complete your WBL, the professionalism and ethics requirements, and the final checks to qualify as a Fellow (FASSA). After qualifying, you maintain your status through annual CPD while you continue to develop in your specialism.

## UK and international: the IFoA route

### 1. Pick your launchpad

You can start with an actuarial degree or almost any strong quantitative degree, such as mathematics, statistics, physics, computer science, engineering or finance. The IFoA route is very accessible to students from non-traditional technical backgrounds.

### 2. Join as a student member

Register with the IFoA and apply for exemptions if your degree is accredited. Plan your progression through the Core Principles subjects, which cover:

- Actuarial Statistics (CS)
- Actuarial Mathematics (CM)
- Business (CB)

Together these form the mathematical and business foundation.

### 3. Core stage

After the Core Principles, take the Core Practices subjects (CP1, CP2 and CP3). Throughout this stage, build up the work-based experience the IFoA requires (referred to as PPD, Personal and Professional Development, in older IFoA material) through your job.

### 4. Specialist focus

Choose Specialist Principles (SP) and Specialist Advanced (SA) subjects. Traditionally this means two SP subjects and one SA subject, but check the current options. They define your pathway, for example:

- life insurance
- general insurance
- pensions
- health and care
- risk management
- investment
- banking

### 5. Qualify and maintain

Complete the experience, professionalism and online ethics requirements to become a Fellow (FIA). After qualifying, keep up CPD to retain your professional status and stay current in your field.

## Planning tips

- **Pace yourself.** Qualification takes several years of study alongside full-time work. A realistic sitting-by-sitting plan beats an ambitious one you abandon.
- **Start the experience requirement early.** It runs in parallel with exams and is easy to fall behind on.
- **Use your employer's study support.** Many graduate programmes offer study leave and pay exam fees, which is worth comparing when you choose between offers.
- **Think about your specialism early.** Your fellowship subjects should match the work you do, or want to do.
